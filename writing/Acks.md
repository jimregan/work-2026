The remainder of these acknowledgements will be a departure from the usual thanks. My (admittedly skewed) impression of printed theses is that the first thing everyone does is to turn to the acknowledgements to see how (or if) they've been mentioned, and I want to play with that.

At the CLARIN conference in 2025, through a mixture of tourism and my usual travel misadventures, I was delayed and missed the first poster session. Afterwards, I bumped into my friend Mícheál, who had met David House at the poster session I'd missed. He asked me if I knew what two things David most associated me with; the answer: funny t-shirts, and cookies.

As odd a pair of things to be associated with they were, I couldn't deny them. For one, I was at that very moment wearing a funny t-shirt. For the other, he wondered if I were bringing that from my time in Ailbhe's lab, but I think she had brought Swedish fika culture from her own time in TMH. But those four words on a postcard would remind me of my time here better than most, and for the rest of these acknowledgements, I want to give the same: a postcard of sorts, a reminder of something from this time.

I'm tempted to be maximally cryptic, so as well as being a reminder to the person in question, it might prompt enough curiosity in others to ask them for the story--Anna: "Axe?"; Anya: hematogen; Ambika: Post-it; Jens: Arlanda; Charlotte: shiny f---ing nose; Éva: free jazz and wild wine; Harm: Linus Torvalds; Shivam: "Gym?" "What?"; 

I'm not going to do that. In keeping with my personal brand of gregarious misanthropy, I'm going to try to maximise the amount of text everyone has to wade through to get to their own mention. Somewhere along the line it seemed to me that a lot of what I was thinking of involved the look on someone's face, so I decided to commit to the bit.

As this was prompted by David, I'll start with a recommendation: if you ever get the chance to be at two poster sessions with David, I recommend missing the first. It's like having your own hype man: every single person whose work related in any way to mine knew about me from David, and because of the positive association from David's positivity and friendliness, I've never been so completely surrounded by people who wanted to meet me. 10/10, would recommend.

Gustav messaged me the last time he was in Dublin to ask if I wanted anything. The first thing that came to mind was the intangible feeling of going to a deli counter and getting a sandwich made: he was staying close enough to the gay Spar[1] which has arguably the best deli section in Dublin, so I recommended there. I would have been happy with a one line report; he sent a video. But the thing I'll always remember is the chocolate. The intangible wasn't enough, Gustav asked again if I wanted something tangible. So I asked for a big bar of Dairy Milk, thinking of the 180g bar. I forgot that there's a 300g bar. He presented me with two. Then, a little later, a third. When I least expected it, there was a fourth, and this time final, bar. My instinct to protest such an excessive degree of generosity was disarmed by the flair.

I don't know if he wants to be reminded of this, but the thing that keeps coming to mind with Jonas is the minor adventure of collecting Birger's theses. There were a lot, so I asked if he'd be able to drive me to collect them. My Swedish is quite rudimentary, but I was able to catch "Har du en bil?" and could figure out from context that the next sentence was directions to the loading bay. I'm sure I mentioned how many boxes there were, but I think the mention of the loading bay pushed that number out of his head. We pulled up there, and got out as she came through the door pushing a trolley with what seemed like all of the theses they had for collection. I'm thankful for three things: that I knew enough Swedish to understand the ambiguity of "De(t) inte alla", that I remembered the number of boxes, and that I got to witness the look on his face that suggested he didn't remember.

Also, shout out to the elevator repair people for the additional exercise that prepared me for it.
