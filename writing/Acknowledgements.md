
To be perfectly honest, I don't fully understand the purpose of these. I've spent most of my life trying to reverse engineer social interactions, so I'll just think out loud a little.

Enough of the other PhDs who I shared my time with have defended that certain patterns emerge. One thing I suspect strongly is that most people write these at the very end of the writing process, when they're already burned out from the writing process, and they end up being one more obstacle before they can finally say they're done.

For most people, the acknowledgements are the first, and often only, part of the thesis that they read, I assume looking for their own name (that's what I do). From the times I've seen others read them, the reaction is usually "oh", which does a lot of heavy lifting. I've heard a tone of pleasant surprise once or twice, but usually I take it to mean "well, it is what it is".

The way acknowledgements look to me is very much reminiscent of speech: they start with a lot of energy, so the thanks to the supervisor is usually quite effusive; this makes a lot of sense, because in the culmination of the academic part, the supervisor is the one who shared most of the journey. Next come the other faculty members who played more key roles in the research that goes into the thesis: typically still quite effusive, but there is a noticeable drop in energy.
