The best-known kind of web crawlers are those used by search engines. More commonly for linguistic purposes, the types of crawler used are more targeted.

One small-scale example is the Corpus Crawler project, an unofficial Google tool that was used to collect text in a variety of predominantly minority languages. Unlike a web-search-style crawler, which tends to follow all links, subject to crawling restrictions (`robots.txt`) and other considerations, Corpus Crawler typically follows a set of index pages, from which it gathers content pages, which it downloads and extracts the text from.

For many multimedia crawlers, the workflow is essentially the same, but instead of, or in addition to, extracting text, the media—audio or video—is also extracted and downloaded.

This introduces extra complexities:

- format handling
    
- alignment
    
- feature extraction, etc.
