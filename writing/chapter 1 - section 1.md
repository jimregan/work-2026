Most PhDs begin with a preliminary title. It's fully expected that this will change: over the course of the PhD, the focus of the work will evolve to follow the direction of the research, but nevertheless the working title marks a starting point, even if the bulk of the later work has followed a different path.

The preliminary title of this PhD was "Automatic Speech Recognition for Swedish". This initial title still represents a thread that connects much of the work contained in this thesis, although the connection is sometimes tenuous. Over the course of the work, it became clear that the use of phonetic transcription had taken a more central role in the thesis.

The aim of this chapter is to present this work in popular science terms, to provide a high-level overview of the work that's intended for a more general audience.

The first part of this chapter will explain in broader terms the background needed to understand the overview of the work itself.

A PhD is guided by many factors; I've already mentioned two of the common threads in this one, but many others have influenced it. One factor is the source of funding: this PhD had funding from two projects: Språkbanken Tal, and SweTerror.

Språkbanken Tal is the division of Språkbanken that is concerned with speech; Språkbanken is the Swedish national infrastructure supporting research in language technology, linguistics, and other areas that conduct research with language data as a basis. Consequently, this PhD has come to have a broader focus than speech recognition alone.

SweTerror is a strongly inter-disciplinary project that investigates how terrorism has been debated in Sweden. This, too, has influenced this PhD: the most central source of data for most of the PhD has been the parliamentary transcripts of Riksdag, the Swedish parliament. It has also shaped many of the initial goals: it is not enough to know what was said, it is also important to know _how_ it was said. To this end, the goals of both funding sources had common ground: the broader speech science goals of Språkbanken Tal find concrete application in SweTerror, as the aspects of speech that are discarded as noise in pure transcription efforts are useful to both.
